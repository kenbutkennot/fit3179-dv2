Malaysia's weight problem: who, where and how fast

Adult obesity rose in every Malaysian state from 2011 to 2019. See where it is highest and how it relates to everyday habits.

By Siaw Boon Ken · Monash University Malaysia · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · 14 October 2026
The headline

More than one in five Malaysian adults has obesity

In 2023, 21.8% of adults had obesity and 32.6% were overweight. Obesity means a body mass index (BMI) of 30 or more. Rates were higher among women (26.0%) than men (17.9%).

Earlier surveys show more detail. In 2019, 28.3% of women aged 45 to 64 had obesity. The squares compare this with 2024 figures for adults and adolescents who were overweight or obese.

Sources: NHMS 2019 (data.gov.my), NHMS 2024 Nutrition Key Findings and NHMS 2023 Technical Report. The 2024 values are rounded estimates.

Where

Women are more affected than men in most states

Darker colours show higher obesity rates. Women's rates are higher in 13 of the 15 states and territories.

Values are simple, unweighted averages of four age groups (18–24, 25–44, 45–64 and 65+). W.P. Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya are too small to see. Hover for exact values.

Melaka has the widest gap (29.8% for women, 10.1% for men). Men have higher rates only in Putrajaya and Pulau Pinang. Putrajaya's small population makes its estimates less stable.

How fast

Every state went up, but some much faster

Rates rose in all 15 states and territories from 2011 to 2019. Negeri Sembilan and Perak rose most (7.3 points), followed by Putrajaya (7.2). Selangor rose least (0.4).

Percentage-point change is the difference between two rates: 15% to 18% is a rise of 3 points.

Perlis led in both 2011 and 2019 (23.1%). Putrajaya and Negeri Sembilan briefly overtook it in 2015. Perak rose from eighth to fourth place.

Where two states have the same rate, the chart lists them one after the other.

Sabah and Pulau Pinang still have the lowest rates, though both have risen.

Who

Middle-aged women stand out

Women have higher rates in every age group. The gap grows from about 4 points at ages 18–24 to 14 points at ages 45–64 (28.3% vs 14.0%).

Women's rates rose in every age group from 2011 to 2019, most among those aged 25–44 (18.6% to 27.6%). The rate fell for men aged 45–64 from 2015 to 2019.

This heatmap breaks every state down by age group.

The latest figures

Perlis and Negeri Sembilan lead in 2023

The 2023 survey measured height and weight in every state. Obesity was highest in Perlis (32.8%), Negeri Sembilan (28.9%) and Putrajaya (25.8%). It was lowest in Kuala Lumpur (18.2%), Selangor (19.1%) and Sabah (19.5%).

These sample estimates have margins of error. Hover for each likely range; Perlis, for example, is 27.1%–39.1%. Smaller states have wider ranges. These values are not comparable with earlier age-group averages.

National obesity rose from 15.1% in 2011 to 21.8% in 2023. Overweight, abdominal obesity and low fruit and vegetable intake also increased.

Source: NHMS 2023 Technical Report, Tables 4.4.1, 4.5.1 and 4.12.1. Abdominal obesity is a waist of 90 cm or more for men and 80 cm or more for women. "Too few fruit and veg" means fewer than five servings a day.

What goes with it?

Habits matter, but no single habit explains the state differences

The 2023 survey measured activity, sitting, diet, water, blood pressure and cholesterol. Across 16 states, links with obesity are weak. Perlis has the highest obesity but below-average inactivity (25.2% vs 29.9%). Nearly all states report low fruit and vegetable intake, so it does little to distinguish them.

With 16 states and wide margins of error, these charts show patterns, not causes. Blood pressure and cholesterol are age-standardised. Source: NHMS 2023 Technical Report, Tables 4.2.4, 4.3.6, 4.8.2, 4.8.4, 4.12.3 and 4.12.4.

The scorecard compares each measure. Dark rows show states with higher rates across several measures.

Colour ranks each state from lowest (0%) to highest (100%); numbers show actual rates. Darker does not always mean worse.

Young people

Young people's habits look worse still

A 2024 survey found about one in three adults and three in five adolescents were inactive. Adolescents did worse on all six habits shown.

Fruit and vegetable bars show the share missing the daily recommendation (2 fruit and 3 vegetable servings). Inactivity and free-sugar values are rounded booklet estimates. Free sugar means over 7.5 teaspoons daily. NHMS 2023 reported low fruit intake (88%) and vegetable intake (91%); surveys differ, so figures are approximate and not directly comparable. National figures do not show what causes obesity.
