Malaysia's weight problem: who, where and how fast

Adult obesity rose in every Malaysian state between 2011 and 2019. These charts show who and where it affects most, and how everyday habits fit in.

By Siaw Boon Ken · Monash University Malaysia · FIT3179 Data Visualisation 2 · 14 October 2026
The headline

More than one in five Malaysian adults has obesity

In the 2023 national health survey, 21.8% of adults had obesity, and another 32.6% were overweight. Together that is more than half of all adults. Obesity here means a body mass index (BMI) of 30 or more. BMI compares a person's weight with their height. Women are affected more: 26.0% of women compared with 17.9% of men.

Earlier surveys let us look closer. In 2019, 28.3% of women aged 45 to 64 were living with obesity. The squares below show what that looks like, next to the overweight-or-obese figures from the 2024 nutrition survey.

Sources: NHMS 2019 (data.gov.my) for the left panel, and NHMS 2024 Nutrition Key Findings for the other two. The 2024 figures are rounded fractions from the survey booklet. Headline figures in the text are from the NHMS 2023 Technical Report.

Where

Women are more affected than men in most states

Darker colours mean a higher share of adults with obesity. The women's map is darker than the men's in 13 of the 15 states and territories shown.

Each state value is the simple average of four age groups (18–24, 25–44, 45–64 and 65+). It is not weighted by population. W.P. Kuala Lumpur and W.P. Putrajaya are too small to see on this map. Hover over a state for exact values.

Melaka has the biggest gap: 29.8% of women compared with 10.1% of men. W.P. Putrajaya (25.5% of men against 19.1% of women) and Pulau Pinang (14.6% against 14.0%) are the only places where men are higher. Putrajaya's population is small, so its figures can swing more.

How fast

Every state went up, but some much faster

Between 2011 and 2019 the average rate rose in all 15 states and territories. The biggest increases were in Negeri Sembilan and Perak (7.3 percentage points each) and W.P. Putrajaya (7.2). The smallest was in Selangor (0.4).

A percentage point is the simple difference between two percentages. For example, going from 15% to 18% is a rise of three percentage points.

Perlis had the highest rate in 2011 and has the highest again in 2019, at 23.1%. In between, Putrajaya and Negeri Sembilan overtook it in 2015. Perak climbed from eighth place in 2011 to fourth in 2019.

Where two states have the same rate, the chart lists them one after the other.

Sabah and Pulau Pinang still have the lowest rates, but both have risen as well.

Who

Middle-aged women stand out

Nationally, women have higher rates than men in every age group. The difference grows with age: about 4 points among 18 to 24 year olds, and about 14 points among those aged 45 to 64 (28.3% against 14.0%).

Among women, every age group is higher in 2019 than in 2011, with the largest rise among those aged 25 to 44 (from 18.6% to 27.6%). Among men aged 45 to 64, the rate fell between 2015 and 2019.

This heatmap breaks every state down by age group.

Some cells are based on small samples. A few show 0.0% or values below 1% (for example, women aged 65 and over in Putrajaya), which most likely means that very few people were surveyed rather than that nobody had obesity.

The latest figures

Perlis and Negeri Sembilan lead in 2023

The 2023 survey measured people's height and weight in every state. Perlis had the highest obesity rate (32.8%), followed by Negeri Sembilan (28.9%) and W.P. Putrajaya (25.8%). The lowest were W.P. Kuala Lumpur (18.2%), Selangor (19.1%) and Sabah (19.5%).

Survey results are estimates from a sample, so each state has a margin of error. Hover over a state to see its likely range. Perlis, for example, is between 27.1% and 39.1%. Smaller states have wider ranges. These state values come from the survey report and are not comparable with the earlier maps, which use simple averages of age groups.

Looking at the whole country, obesity rose from 15.1% in 2011 to 21.8% in 2023. Overweight, belly fat (a large waist) and a lack of fruit and vegetables also rose.

Source: NHMS 2023 Technical Report, Tables 4.4.1, 4.5.1 and 4.12.1. Belly fat means a waist of 90 cm or more for men and 80 cm or more for women. "Too few fruit and veg" means fewer than five servings a day.

What goes with it?

Habits matter, but no single habit explains the state differences

It is natural to ask whether states with more obesity also have worse habits. The 2023 survey also measured physical activity, sitting time, diet, water intake, blood pressure and cholesterol. Across the 16 states, the links with obesity are loose. Perlis has the highest obesity rate but lower-than-average inactivity (25.2% against 29.9% nationally), and in almost every state 90% or more of adults eat too few fruit and vegetables, so that habit cannot separate one state from another.

With only 16 states and sizeable margins of error, these charts can show patterns but not causes. Blood pressure and cholesterol values are age-standardised, so states with different age mixes can be compared fairly. Source: NHMS 2023 Technical Report, Tables 4.2.4, 4.3.6, 4.8.2, 4.8.4, 4.12.3 and 4.12.4.

The scorecard puts the measures side by side. Rows that are dark all the way across are states with relatively high rates on many measures at once.

Colour shows how a state ranks among the 16 states on each measure (0% = lowest rate, 100% = highest). The printed number is the actual percentage. A dark cell means a relatively high rate, which is not always the same as a worse result.

Young people

Young people's habits look worse still

A separate 2024 nutrition survey asked adults and adolescents about the same daily habits. About one in three adults is not physically active. Among adolescents the figure is about three in five. On all six habits shown here, adolescents fare worse than adults.

The fruit and vegetable bars show 100% minus the share who met the daily recommendation (2 servings of fruit, 3 of vegetables). "Not physically active" and "free sugar" use rounded fractions from the booklet (1 in 3 and 3 in 5; 1 in 2 and 2 in 3). Free sugar means more than 7.5 teaspoons a day. The 2023 report found that 88% of adults ate too little fruit and 91% too little vegetables, which is slightly worse than the 2024 booklet implies. The two surveys are different, so treat the figures as roughly similar, not exactly comparable. These are national figures, and they do not prove what causes obesity.
